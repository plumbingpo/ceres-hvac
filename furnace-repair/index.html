From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, the sequence is strict: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower sending heat through the house. Diagnosis is mostly a matter of finding where that chain breaks. In Ceres homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Rather than guess, Ceres HVAC tests the sequence one stage at a time. We read igniter resistance, measure flame signal in microamps, check that the pressure switch responds to real inducer draft, confirm voltage at the gas valve, and test the blower and its capacitor. Because this is a fuel-burning appliance, the safety basics come with every visit: we inspect the heat exchanger, leak-test the gas connections, and verify flue draft. You cannot tell a furnace problem from a furnace hazard by looking at the thermostat. Before we leave, temperature rise is measured against the data plate so airflow and flame are matched as the manufacturer intended.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we repair them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are already stocked on the truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. Signs You Need Furnace Repair in Ceres

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • A pilot or burner flame that is lazy yellow instead of crisp blue
  • Short cycling every few minutes without reaching the setpoint
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • The furnace clicks and glows but the burners never light
  • Soot, rust, or moisture near the furnace cabinet or vent pipe
  • Squealing or grinding from the blower compartment
  • Booming or banging at light-off from delayed ignition
  • Repeated shutdowns just seconds after the burners light

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

A furnace combines gas, flame, high voltage, and a flue in one cabinet, and the failure that matters most is the one you cannot see from outside: a compromised heat exchanger passing combustion gases into the air your blower pushes through the house. Delayed ignition that booms at light-off stresses that exchanger further every morning it goes unfixed. Meanwhile the diagnostic traps stack up: a furnace that trips its high limit may have a bad switch, or it may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a dying blower, and replacing the switch in the second case just removes a safety while leaving the fault.

At Ceres HVAC, no symptom gets treated in isolation. A meter tells us the flame signal, so we never condemn a good sensor while the real culprit, a cracked igniter or a weak gas valve, keeps causing trouble. We confirm draft at both the pressure switch and the flue, measure temperature rise against the data plate to flag airflow problems early, and leak-test every gas connection with instruments, not a guess. When we are done, the furnace has proven itself safe and correct through a full cycle, and if it is nearing the end of its useful life, you will get an honest assessment.

Maintenance Tips for Furnace Repair

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Avoid packing the utility room, because burners need open space for combustion air
  • Change the filter monthly during heating season; it is the cheapest furnace part you will ever buy
  • Listen at light-off; clean ignition is quiet, and booming means the burners need service
  • Keep the condensate trap and drain clear on high-efficiency units to prevent lockouts
  • Schedule service each fall to catch worn igniters and dirty sensors before cold weather arrives
  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
  • Clear boxes, lint, and above all chlorine-based cleaners out of the furnace area
